# Capability Trajectory & Economic Impact: Detailed Data

## November 2026 evidence

*Updated November 1, 2026. The November 2026 section is current. Everything after it is the December 2025 compilation, kept as dated history. Superlatives and words like "latest", "leading" or "now" in that older text describe December 2025.*

- McKinsey's AI value curve (Oct 14, 2026) estimates a roughly $2.5 trillion annual global opportunity, $2.52 trillion, from deploying AI on tasks where it is cheaper than current work, and says a 30% rise in AI cost would cut the global pool from $2.52 trillion to $1.67 trillion. ([McKinsey, Oct 14, 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-ai-value-curve-the-economics-of-applying-ai-to-existing-work)) *Method: Model-based estimate, not a measured effect, McKinsey says it 'does not model organizational, regulatory, or workforce constraints'.*
- Google's Gemini 4 Argon model card lists Terminal-bench 4.0 at 57.4% for Argon, 58.2% for GPT-6 Astra, 57.9% for Claude Fable 5.1 and 66.4% for Claude Opus 5.5, while OpenAI's and Anthropic's pages give Fable 5.1 as 55.8% and Astra as 57.9%; the same model gets different scores in different vendors' tables. ([Google DeepMind, Oct 13, 2026](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-4-argon/); [OpenAI, GPT-6 Astra page, undated](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/); [Anthropic, Sep 22, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)) *Method: Vendor-reported by Google.*
- Federal Reserve Governor Cook said on Sept 28, 2026 that there is limited evidence that AI is changing the structure of the labor market so far, with unemployment and layoffs low and flat over two years, and that AI-related demand is adding some inflation pressure. ([Federal Reserve, Sep 28, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20260928a.htm)) *Method: Policy speech, not a study, the same speech cites the Fed Small Business Credit Survey: 'nearly half of small employer firms are using AI and that 71 percent report increased productivity as a result' (self-reported).*
- METR states its time-horizon page is no longer actively updated (last updated Sept 8, 2026), and its update log lists no model after Claude Mythos Preview (early), added May 8, 2026. ([METR, Sep 8, 2026](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)) *Method: The absence of Opus 5.5 / GPT-6 rows is inferred from the page's update log (latest model entry: Claude Mythos Preview (early), May 8, 2026).*
- The Dallas Fed (Jul 7, 2026) reports U.S. labor productivity grew at a 2.4 percent annualized rate since the start of 2024 against 1.6 percent in the five pre-pandemic years, and 3.7 percent in the three most AI-exposed sectors against 1.7 percent in the rest of the economy. ([Dallas Fed, Jul 7, 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0707)) *Method: Correlation between an AI-exposure index (Felten, Raj, Seamans) and industry productivity growth, the Dallas Fed uses cross-country data to probe causation but the first paragraph states the open question 'is AI itself responsible for that growth'.*
- Anthropic's June 2026 Economic Index report states that large majorities of people report productivity gains in speed (86%), scope (82%) and quality (69%) of their work, based on interviews with Claude users. ([Anthropic, Jun 26, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)) *Method: Self-reported by Claude users who agreed to be interviewed (interviews conducted Dec 2025 per the report), not a measured productivity effect.*
- PwC's 2026 AI Jobs Barometer (15 June 2026) reports that productivity growth is 40% higher at companies most exposed to AI than at those least exposed, and that the top fifth of most-exposed companies averaged 163% productivity growth since 2022. ([PwC, Jun 15, 2026](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/services/ai/ai-jobs-barometer.html)) *Method: Correlational comparison of exposed vs unexposed companies by a consulting firm.*
- Epoch AI released a major FrontierMath update on 2026-06-12 that fixed errors in 42% of problems and split the set into 295 Tier 1-3 and 43 Tier 4 problems, so scores on earlier versions are not comparable with v2 scores. ([Epoch AI, Jun 12, 2026](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath)) *Method: Benchmark owner page.*
- The San Francisco Fed (May 26, 2026) says incoming productivity data have been mixed: labor productivity has risen solidly while its total factor productivity index has grown only modestly, so the data do not yet show a 1990s-style high-productivity regime. ([San Francisco Fed, May 26, 2026](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2026/05/have-we-entered-era-of-high-productivity-growth/)) *Method: Regime-switching model on U.S. business-sector labor productivity and TFP, the letter offers 'cautious optimism'.*
- METR's data file gives Claude Mythos Preview (early) a 50% time horizon of about 1,045 minutes (about 17.4 hours), above the 16-hour level where METR says its current task suite is unreliable. ([METR, May 8, 2026](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)) *Method: Estimate from METR's data file, in minutes.*
- An NBER working paper (issue date March 2026) based on a survey of nearly 750 corporate executives finds positive labor productivity gains, a 'productivity paradox' in which perceived gains exceed measured gains, and little evidence of near-term aggregate employment declines from AI. ([NBER, Mar 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34984)) *Method: Survey of nearly 750 US corporate executives (authors from Federal Reserve Banks of Atlanta and Richmond and Duke), self-reported, not administrative data.*
- METR's data file gives Claude Opus 4.6 (released 2026-02-05) a 50% time horizon of about 719 minutes (about 12 hours), with a 95% interval of about 317 to 3,634 minutes. ([METR, Feb 6, 2026](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)) *Method: Estimate from METR's data file, in minutes.*
- METR's Time Horizon 1.1 post (Jan 29, 2026) reports that the doubling time of the 50% time horizon since 2023 is 131 days on the new task suite versus 165 days on the original, and about 196 days (7 months) across 2019-2025. ([METR, Jan 29, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/)) *Method: Software, ML and cybersecurity tasks only. METR says the trend is sensitive to the task suite.*
- The IMF said in its Jan 21, 2026 WEO Update press conference that, if tech-boom productivity gains materialize, it estimates global output could increase by 0.3 percent in 2026. ([IMF, Jan 21, 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/01/21/tr-01212026-weo-press-conference-on-release-of-the-january-2026-world-economic-outlook-update)) *Method: Scenario estimate stated by IMF staff in a press conference, not a measurement.*
- Epoch AI's own runs on the private FrontierMath Tier 4 (v2) set give GPT-6 Astra 97.6% (+/-2) at xhigh effort (run 2026-08-30) and GPT-6.1 Sol 100% at max effort (run 2026-09-29). ## Economic Impact Projections

### [McKinsey Global Institute](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier)

**Key Findings**:
- Generative AI could add $2.6-4.4 trillion annually to global economy
- Equivalent to adding entire UK GDP
- 75% of value in four areas:
  1. Customer operations
  2. Marketing and sales
  3. Software engineering
  4. R&D

**Labor Impact**:
- Could automate 60-70% of current work activities
- Accelerate labor productivity growth by 0.1-0.6%/year
- Could add 0.5-3.4% to global GDP growth annually

### PricewaterhouseCoopers (PWC)

**Projections**:
- AI contribution to global GDP: $15.7 trillion by 2030
- Breakdown:
  - Productivity gains: $6.6 trillion
  - Consumption effects: $9.1 trillion
- Largest gains: China (26% GDP boost), North America (14%)

### [International Monetary Fund (IMF)](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/04/04/ai-adoption-and-inequality-565729)

**Key Findings**:
- ~40% of global employment exposed to AI
- Advanced economies: 60% exposure (mixed impact)
- Emerging markets: 40% exposure (fewer benefits initially)
- Low-income countries: 26% exposure (risk of widening gap)

**Inequality Concerns**:
- AI may increase income and wealth inequality
- High-skill workers benefit most from augmentation
- Lower-skill workers face displacement risk

### Near-Term (2025-2026)

## Uncertainty and Caveats

### Factors That Could Accelerate
- Breakthrough architectures
- Compute cost reductions
- Data availability improvements
- Competition intensification

### Factors That Could Slow
- Safety regulations
- Compute constraints
- Diminishing returns on current approaches
- Economic or geopolitical disruption

### Projection Reliability
- Near-term (1 year): High confidence
- Medium-term (2-3 years): Moderate confidence
- Long-term (5+ years): Low confidence, directionally indicative only

## Implications for University Planning

### Curriculum Cycle Mismatch
- Traditional curriculum cycle: 5-7 years
- AI capability doubling time: ~14 months
- Gap: 4-5 capability doublings per curriculum cycle

### Required Adaptations
1. Continuous curriculum updating (annual or faster)
2. Modular course design for rapid updates
3. AI literacy as foundational requirement
4. Skills focus over knowledge focus
5. Adaptability and learning agility emphasis
