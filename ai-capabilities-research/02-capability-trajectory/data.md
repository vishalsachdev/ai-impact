# Capability Trajectory & Economic Impact: Detailed Data

## November 2026 evidence

*Updated November 1, 2026. Evidence through October 2026. Each item carries an evidence label, explained on the home page.*

- **Consultancy estimate.** McKinsey's AI value curve (Oct 14, 2026) estimates a roughly $2.5 trillion annual global opportunity, $2.52 trillion, from deploying AI on tasks where it is cheaper than current work, and says a 30% rise in AI cost would cut the global pool from $2.52 trillion to $1.67 trillion. ([McKinsey, Oct 14, 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-ai-value-curve-the-economics-of-applying-ai-to-existing-work)) *Method: Model-based estimate, not a measured effect, McKinsey says it 'does not model organizational, regulatory, or workforce constraints'.*
- **Vendor-reported.** Google's Gemini 4 Argon model card lists Terminal-bench 4.0 at 57.4% for Argon, 58.2% for GPT-6 Astra, 57.9% for Claude Fable 5.1 and 66.4% for Claude Opus 5.5. Anthropic's Opus 5.5 page gives Fable 5.1 as 55.8% and Astra as 57.9%, so the same models carry different scores in different vendors' tables, possibly because of effort settings or harnesses. ([Google DeepMind, Oct 13, 2026](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-4-argon/); [OpenAI, GPT-6 Astra page, undated](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/); [Anthropic, Sep 22, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)) *Method: Vendor-reported by Google.*
- **Official source.** Federal Reserve Governor Cook said on Sept 28, 2026 that there is limited evidence that AI is changing the structure of the labor market so far, with unemployment and layoffs low and flat over two years, and that AI-related demand is adding some inflation pressure. ([Federal Reserve, Sep 28, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20260928a.htm)) *Method: Policy speech, not a study, the same speech cites the Fed Small Business Credit Survey: 'nearly half of small employer firms are using AI and that 71 percent report increased productivity as a result' (self-reported).*
- **Third-party measurement.** METR states its time-horizon page is no longer actively updated (last updated Sept 8, 2026), and its update log lists no model after Claude Mythos Preview (early), added May 8, 2026. ([METR, Sep 8, 2026](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)) *Method: The absence of Opus 5.5 / GPT-6 rows is inferred from the page's update log (latest model entry: Claude Mythos Preview (early), May 8, 2026).*
- **Official source.** The Dallas Fed (Jul 7, 2026) reports U.S. labor productivity grew at a 2.4 percent annualized rate since the start of 2024 against 1.6 percent in the five pre-pandemic years, and 3.7 percent in the three most AI-exposed sectors against 1.7 percent in the rest of the economy. ([Dallas Fed, Jul 7, 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0707)) *Method: Correlation between an AI-exposure index (Felten, Raj, Seamans) and industry productivity growth, the Dallas Fed uses cross-country data to probe causation but the first paragraph states the open question 'is AI itself responsible for that growth'.*
- **Vendor-reported.** Anthropic's June 2026 Economic Index report states that large majorities of people report productivity gains in speed (86%), scope (82%) and quality (69%) of their work, based on interviews with Claude users. ([Anthropic, Jun 26, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)) *Method: Self-reported by Claude users who agreed to be interviewed (interviews conducted Dec 2025 per the report), not a measured productivity effect.*
- **Consultancy estimate.** PwC's 2026 AI Jobs Barometer (15 June 2026) reports that productivity growth is 40% higher at companies most exposed to AI than at those least exposed, and that the top fifth of most-exposed companies averaged 163% productivity growth since 2022. ([PwC, Jun 15, 2026](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/services/ai/ai-jobs-barometer.html)) *Method: Correlational comparison of exposed vs unexposed companies by a consulting firm.*
- **Third-party measurement.** Epoch AI released a major FrontierMath update on 2026-06-12 that addressed errors in 42% of problems. The v2 dataset has 338 problems: 295 in Tiers 1-3 and 43 in Tier 4. ([Epoch AI, Jun 12, 2026](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath)) *Method: Benchmark owner page.*
- **Official source.** The San Francisco Fed (May 26, 2026) says incoming productivity data have been mixed: labor productivity has risen solidly while its total factor productivity index has grown only modestly, so the data do not yet show a 1990s-style high-productivity regime. ([San Francisco Fed, May 26, 2026](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2026/05/have-we-entered-era-of-high-productivity-growth/)) *Method: Regime-switching model on U.S. business-sector labor productivity and TFP, the letter offers 'cautious optimism'.*
- **Third-party measurement.** METR's data file gives Claude Mythos Preview (early) a 50% time horizon of about 1,045 minutes (about 17.4 hours), above the 16-hour level where METR says its current task suite is unreliable. ([METR, May 8, 2026](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)) *Method: Estimate from METR's data file, in minutes.*
- **Unreviewed paper.** An NBER working paper (issue date March 2026) based on a survey of nearly 750 corporate executives finds positive labor productivity gains, a 'productivity paradox' in which perceived gains exceed measured gains, and little evidence of near-term aggregate employment declines from AI. ([NBER, Mar 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34984)) *Method: Survey of nearly 750 US corporate executives (authors from Federal Reserve Banks of Atlanta and Richmond and Duke), self-reported, not administrative data.*
- **Third-party measurement.** METR's data file gives Claude Opus 4.6 (released 2026-02-05) a 50% time horizon of about 719 minutes (about 12 hours), with a 95% interval of about 317 to 3,634 minutes. ([METR, Feb 6, 2026](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)) *Method: Estimate from METR's data file, in minutes.*
- **Third-party measurement.** METR's Time Horizon 1.1 post (Jan 29, 2026) reports that the doubling time of the 50% time horizon since 2023 is 131 days on the new task suite versus 165 days on the original, and about 196 days (7 months) across 2019-2025. ([METR, Jan 29, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/)) *Method: Software, ML and cybersecurity tasks only. METR says the trend is sensitive to the task suite.*
- **Official source.** The IMF said in its Jan 21, 2026 WEO Update press conference that, if tech-boom productivity gains materialize, it estimates global output could increase by 0.3 percent in 2026. ([IMF, Jan 21, 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/01/21/tr-01212026-weo-press-conference-on-release-of-the-january-2026-world-economic-outlook-update)) *Method: Scenario estimate stated by IMF staff in a press conference, not a measurement.*
- **Third-party measurement.** Epoch AI's own runs on the private FrontierMath Tier 4 (v2) set give GPT-6 Astra 97.6% (+/-2) at xhigh effort (run 2026-08-30) and GPT-6.1 Sol 100% at max effort (run 2026-09-29). OpenAI reported 14.6% for GPT-5.2 in December 2025 on the earlier Tier 4 set. The two versions differ, so these scores are not like for like. ([Epoch AI, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmarks.csv); [OpenAI, Dec 11, 2025](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-2/)) *Method: Independent evaluation by the benchmark owner on 43 Tier 4 problems, so one problem is about 2.3 points and the ceiling is close, v1 vs v2 not comparable.*
- **Third-party measurement.** Epoch AI's own runs on the private FrontierMath Tier 1-3 (v2) set give Claude Opus 5.5 91.2%, GPT-6 Astra 93.7% and GPT-6.1 Sol 93.7%. OpenAI reported 40.3% for GPT-5.2 in December 2025 on the earlier set. The two versions differ, so these scores are not like for like. ([Epoch AI, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmarks.csv); [OpenAI, Dec 11, 2025](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-2/)) *Method: Independent evaluation, Tier 1-3 v2 has 295 problems.*
- **Expert compilation.** Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports that agent accuracy on OSWorld rose from roughly 12% to 66.3%, within 6 percentage points of human performance, and that agents still fail about one in three attempts on structured benchmarks. ([Stanford HAI, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/technical-performance)) *Method: OSWorld has changed version since: Anthropic's Opus 5.5 page (2026-09-22) reports 'OSWorld 2.1 81.8% partial' and OpenAI's Astra page 72.6% on 'OSWorld 2.0 (v2026.08.08, offline set, partial score)', these are different task sets and scoring.*
- **Expert compilation.** Stanford's 2026 AI Index says SWE-bench Verified performance rose from 60% to near 100% in a single year, and that industry produced over 90% of notable frontier models in 2025. ([Stanford HAI, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report)) *Method: Compilation.*
- **Expert compilation.** Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports employment for software developers aged 22 to 25 fell nearly 20% from 2024, and that studies show productivity gains of 14% to 15% in customer support, 26% in software development and 50% in marketing output. ([Stanford HAI, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy)) *Method: Compilation of other authors' studies, different populations and years, the Index says gains are 'smaller in tasks requiring deeper reasoning' and raises.*
