# Capability Trajectory & Economic Impact

## November 2026 update

*Updated November 1, 2026. The November 2026 section is current. Everything after it is the December 2025 compilation, kept as dated history. Superlatives and words like "latest", "leading" or "now" in that older text describe December 2025.*

- METR's Time Horizon 1.1 post (Jan 29, 2026) reports that the doubling time of the 50% time horizon since 2023 is 131 days on the new task suite versus 165 days on the original, and about 196 days (7 months) across 2019-2025. ([METR, Jan 29, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/))
- METR states its time-horizon page is no longer actively updated (last updated Sept 8, 2026), and its update log lists no model after Claude Mythos Preview (early), added May 8, 2026. ([METR, Sep 8, 2026](https://metr.org/time-horizons/))
- Epoch AI released a major FrontierMath update on 2026-06-12 that fixed errors in 42% of problems and split the set into 295 Tier 1-3 and 43 Tier 4 problems, so scores on earlier versions are not comparable with v2 scores. ([Epoch AI, Jun 12, 2026](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath))
- Epoch AI's own runs on the private FrontierMath Tier 1-3 (v2) set give Claude Opus 5.5 91.2%, GPT-6 Astra 93.7% and GPT-6.1 Sol 93.7%. OpenAI reported 40.3% for GPT-5.2 in December 2025 on the earlier set; Epoch says scores on the two versions are not comparable. ([Epoch AI, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmarks.csv); [OpenAI, Dec 11, 2025](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-2/))
- McKinsey's AI value curve (Oct 14, 2026) estimates a roughly $2.5 trillion annual global opportunity, $2.52 trillion, from deploying AI on tasks where it is cheaper than current work, and says a 30% rise in AI cost would cut the global pool from $2.52 trillion to $1.67 trillion. ([McKinsey, Oct 14, 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-ai-value-curve-the-economics-of-applying-ai-to-existing-work))
- The Dallas Fed (Jul 7, 2026) reports U.S. labor productivity grew at a 2.4 percent annualized rate since the start of 2024 against 1.6 percent in the five pre-pandemic years, and 3.7 percent in the three most AI-exposed sectors against 1.7 percent in the rest of the economy. ([Dallas Fed, Jul 7, 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0707))
- Federal Reserve Governor Cook said on Sept 28, 2026 that there is limited evidence that AI is changing the structure of the labor market so far, with unemployment and layoffs low and flat over two years, and that AI-related demand is adding some inflation pressure. ([Federal Reserve, Sep 28, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20260928a.htm))

## Research Question

**How fast are AI capabilities improving, and what are the projected economic impacts?**

## Hypothesis

AI capabilities are improving at an accelerating rate that outpaces institutional adaptation cycles, with transformative economic implications requiring proactive university response.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Improvement Rates (2024-2025)

| Model Transition | Timeframe | Improvement | Benchmark | Source |
|------------------|-----------|-------------|-----------|--------|
| GPT-4o → GPT-5 | 14 months | >2x | GDPval expert parity | [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/gdpval/) |
| Gemini 2.5 → 3 | 7 months | 50% | Developer tasks | [Google](https://blog.google/products/gemini/gemini-3/) |
| Claude 4.1 → 4.5 | 3 months | 74.5% → 80.9% | SWE-bench | [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-5) |

### 2. Key Trajectory Observations

- **Linear improvement on GDPval**: Performance improving roughly linearly over time
- **Accelerating release cycles**: Major models released every 3-7 months
- **Cross-lab convergence**: Top models clustering in performance
- **Specialization emerging**: Different models excel at different tasks

### 4. Labor Market Implications

- **40% of global employment** exposed to AI ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/04/04/ai-adoption-and-inequality-565729))
- **High-skill workers** most exposed but also most augmented
- **Emerging economies** potentially disadvantaged by slower adoption

## Implications for Universities

### Strategic Planning
- 3-5 year curriculum cycles may be too slow
- Capability assumptions obsolete within 1-2 years
- Continuous curriculum updating required

### Workforce Preparation
- Students entering workforce face different AI landscape than during training
- Adaptability and AI collaboration skills essential
- Domain expertise remains valuable anchor

### Research Prioritization
- AI capabilities research itself rapidly changing
- Cross-disciplinary AI literacy needed
- Ethics and governance research increasingly urgent
