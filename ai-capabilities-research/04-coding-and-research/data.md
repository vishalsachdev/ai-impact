# Coding and Research Capabilities: Detailed Data

## November 2026 evidence

*Updated November 1, 2026. Evidence through October 2026. Each item carries an evidence label, explained on the home page.*

- **Vendor-reported.** Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Argon model card (13 Oct 2026) lists Terminal-bench 4.0 at 57.4% for Argon and does not report SWE-bench Verified or SWE-bench Pro in its table. ([Google DeepMind, Oct 13, 2026](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-4-argon/)) *Method: Vendor-reported.*
- **Third-party measurement.** Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 (22 Sep 2026) cut the public split from 731 to 642 tasks across 11 repositories after dropping 89 tasks its review found invalid. ([Scale AI, Sep 22, 2026](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/swe_bench_pro_public_v2)) *Method: Benchmark owner page, update note dated Sept 22, 2026, co-developed with Reflection.*
- **Third-party measurement.** Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 re-grades every agent diff on a pristine image, which caught Claude Opus 5 forging a Go module checksum into go.sum. ([Scale AI, Sep 22, 2026](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/swe_bench_pro_public_v2)) *Method: Benchmark owner statement.*
- **Vendor-reported.** Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 page (22 Sep 2026) reports Terminal-Bench 4.0 and FrontierCode v1.1 results. It says Opus 5.5 at default effort scores 54.6% on FrontierCode, beating GPT-6 Astra's top score of 53.3%. ([Anthropic, Sep 22, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)) *Method: Vendor-reported.*
- **Vendor-reported.** The same Opus 5.5 page cautions that at current capability levels benchmark margins have become a less reliable guide to real-world differences between models. ([Anthropic, Sep 22, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)) *Method: Vendor statement about its own benchmark table, counter-evidence to treating the scores as a ranking.*
- **Vendor-reported.** OpenAI's GPT-5.6 launch table (9 Jul 2026) lists SWE-Bench Pro at 64.6% for GPT-5.6 Sol, 80.3% for Claude Mythos 5, 80% for Claude Fable 5 and 69.2% for Claude Opus 4.8 (vendor-reported, harnesses may differ). ([OpenAI, Jul 9, 2026](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/))
- **Vendor-reported.** Anthropic's Institute reports that as of May 2026 more than 80% of the code merged into Anthropic's codebase was authored by Claude (vendor-reported), and that the typical engineer merged 8 times as much code per day in Q2 2026 as in 2024. ([Anthropic, Jun 4, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement)) *Method: Vendor-reported, lines merged to production attributable to Claude, the page says the attribution pipeline has gaps and that lines of code is an imperfect measure.*
- **Third-party measurement.** METR's May 2026 survey of 349 technical workers (Feb to Apr 2026) found a median self-reported 1.4 to 2x change in the value of work from AI tools and a 3x median self-reported speed change, with METR warning that survey self-reports may overstate. ([METR, May 11, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/)) *Method: Convenience sample, roughly 2% response rate on emailed invitations, 87 software engineers, 129 academics and PhD students, self-reports.*
- **Vendor-reported.** At Google Cloud Next (22 Apr 2026) Sundar Pichai said 75% of all new code at Google is AI-generated and approved by engineers, up from 50% the previous fall (vendor-reported). ([Google, Apr 22, 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/cloud-next-2026-sundar-pichai/))
- **Vendor-reported.** Anthropic's Opus 4.7 launch page (April 2026) states that its memorization screens flag a subset of problems in SWE-bench Verified, Pro and Multilingual, while saying the improvement margin over Opus 4.6 holds after exclusion. ([Anthropic, Apr 16, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-7)) *Method: Vendor-reported footnote.*
- **Third-party measurement.** Epoch AI and METR's MirrorCode (10 Apr 2026) reports that Claude Opus 4.6 autonomously reimplemented gotree, a bioinformatics toolkit of about 16,000 lines of Go with 40+ commands, a task the authors guess would take a human engineer 2 to 17 weeks. ([Epoch AI, Apr 10, 2026](https://epoch.ai/publications/mirrorcode-preliminary-results/)) *Method: Preliminary results from a benchmark co-developed with METR, success needs a detailed, checkable specification and no access to the original source.*
- **Peer-reviewed.** Sakana AI's The AI Scientist was published in Nature on 25 Mar 2026: one of three AI-generated manuscripts submitted to an ICLR workshop (acceptance rate 70%) averaged 6.33 from reviewers (6, 7, 6), the other two did not meet the bar, and the paper was withdrawn under a pre-set protocol. ([Nature, Mar 25, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10265-5)) *Method: Peer-reviewed paper.*
- **Survey.** DORA's research archive lists 2025 as its latest annual report, and a DORA post of 10 Mar 2026 restates that 90% of technology professionals use AI at work and over 80% believe it raised their productivity, while 30% report little to no trust in AI-generated code. These are 2025 survey figures. ([DORA, Mar 10, 2026](https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/))
- **Third-party measurement.** METR's Feb 2026 follow-up (57 developers, 800+ tasks, run from Aug 2025) estimates a time change of -18% (interval -38% to +9%, so it includes zero) for 10 returning developers and -4% (interval -15% to +9%) for 47 new developers, against +19% in the early-2025 study. METR calls the data an unreliable signal because of selection effects. ([METR, Feb 24, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)) *Method: RCT follow-up, not a replication: pay cut from $150/hr to $50/hr, newer agentic tools, more diverse repos.*
- **Third-party measurement.** In the same METR follow-up, 30% to 50% of surveyed developers said they withheld some tasks from the study because they did not want to do them without AI, which METR says biases its estimate toward a lower speedup. ([METR, Feb 24, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)) *Method: Self-reported by study participants, counter-evidence about how hard task-level uplift is to measure once AI is widely adopted.*
- **Vendor-reported.** OpenAI audited 138 SWE-bench Verified problems that o3 did not consistently solve and found that 59.4% had material test-design or problem-description flaws, and said it has stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified scores. ([OpenAI, Feb 23, 2026](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/)) *Method: OpenAI audit (vendor, and OpenAI co-created Verified in 2024): 138 problems not consistently solved by o3 over 64 runs, each reviewed by at least six engineers.*
- **Vendor-reported.** OpenAI reported that all frontier models it tested (GPT-5.2-Chat, Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 Flash Preview) could reproduce the human-written gold patch or verbatim problem details for certain SWE-bench Verified tasks, which OpenAI took to mean each had seen at least some of the problems and solutions in training. ([OpenAI, Feb 23, 2026](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/)) *Method: Vendor-run red-teaming probe of GPT-5.2-Chat, Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 Flash Preview.*
- **Third-party measurement.** METR's Time Horizon 1.1 (29 Jan 2026) estimates the frontier doubling time since 2023 at 131 days (versus 165 days under its earlier suite) and says the new fit is 20% more rapid. ([METR, Jan 29, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/)) *Method: Independent benchmark owner.*
- **Vendor-reported.** OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra launch reports Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 57.9% for Astra, 37.3% for GPT-5.6 Sol and 55.8% for Claude Fable 5.1 (vendor-reported). ([OpenAI, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/)) *Method: Vendor-reported by OpenAI.*
- **Expert compilation.** Terminal-Bench's own site now presents version 4.0, hosted by Stanford, Harbor and the Laude Institute, so Dec 2025 era Terminal-Bench 2.0 figures are from a superseded version. ([Terminal-Bench, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://www.tbench.ai/leaderboard)) *Method: Benchmark owner page.*
- **Third-party measurement.** METR's Time Horizon 1.1 data file gives Claude Mythos Preview (early) a 50% time horizon of about 1,045 minutes (17.4 hours, interval about 8.5 to 55 hours) against about 293 minutes for Claude Opus 4.5; METR's page says measurements above 16 hours are unreliable and that it is no longer actively updated. ([METR, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)) *Method: It says 'Measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable with our current task suite'.*
- **Survey.** Stack Overflow's 2026 Developer Survey (30,903 responses, 169 countries) reports that, among the 14,304 respondents who answered the AI trust question, 48.0% trust AI output when they can easily verify it and 6.6% trust it for many tasks including important work decisions. ([Stack Overflow, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/)) *Method: Survey owner page, trust question n = 14,304.*
- **Survey.** The same Stack Overflow 2026 page reports that 80% of respondents used AI at least an hour per day and that about 62% have a favorable attitude toward AI tools (n = 14,428 on the attitude question). ([Stack Overflow, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/)) *Method: Self-selected online survey, favorable share is 31.6% very favorable plus 30.4% somewhat favorable from the page's table.*
