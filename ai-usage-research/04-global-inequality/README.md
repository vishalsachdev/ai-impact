# Research Question 4: Global Inequality in AI Access & Adoption

## November 2026 update

*Updated November 1, 2026. The November 2026 section is current. Everything after it is the December 2025 compilation, kept as dated history. Superlatives and words like "latest", "leading" or "now" in that older text describe December 2025.*

- Microsoft telemetry puts generative AI use at 28.8% of the working-age population in the Global North and 16.2% in the Global South in June 2026 (Q2), a 12.6 point gap, up from 10.6 points in H2 2025. ([Microsoft AI Economy Institute, Sep 21, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Microsoft-AI-Diffusion-Report-2026-Q2.pdf))
- Anthropic's March 2026 report (February 2026 Claude.ai data) says the top 20 countries account for 48% of population-adjusted usage, up from 45%, and that low-adoption countries fell slightly further behind. ([Anthropic, Mar 24, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report))
- Anthropic's January 2026 report (November 2025 Claude.ai data) says cross-country usage per capita is strongly correlated with GDP and that there was no evidence of low-use countries catching up; a 1% rise in GDP per capita goes with 0.7% higher Claude usage per capita. ([Anthropic, Jan 15, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report))
- Anthropic's India brief (November 2025 data) says India accounts for 5.8% of Claude.ai use, second to the U.S., but ranks 101st of 116 countries per capita. ([Anthropic, Feb 16, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/india-brief-economic-index))
- Microsoft's Q2 2026 leaderboard is topped by the UAE (73.3%) and Singapore (64.3%), while the Global South average is 16.2%. ([Microsoft AI Economy Institute, Sep 21, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Microsoft-AI-Diffusion-Report-2026-Q2.pdf))
- ITU estimates 2.2 billion people were offline in 2025, with 94% internet use in high-income countries versus 23% in low-income countries and 77% of men online versus 71% of women. ([ITU, Nov 17, 2025](https://www.itu.int/en/mediacentre/Pages/PR-2025-11-17-facts-and-figures.aspx))

## Primary Question
How is AI adoption distributed globally, and is AI widening or narrowing global inequality gaps?

## Key Hypothesis
Despite rapid growth in emerging economies, AI adoption may paradoxically widen global inequality due to absolute usage gaps, infrastructure barriers, and differential economic impacts.

## Significance
Understanding global inequality in AI is critical for:
- Development policy and international aid priorities
- Predicting economic divergence between nations
- Designing inclusive AI systems and policies
- Infrastructure investment strategies
- Educational equity initiatives

## Key Findings Summary
- **The Paradox**: Growth fastest in poor countries (4x), but absolute usage highest in rich countries ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/))
- **Extreme disparities**: Singapore uses AI 23x more than Nigeria (AUI: 4.6 vs 0.20) ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)) *Corrected November 1, 2026: The 23x compares rounded Claude.ai usage per working-age person (August 2025 data). It does not measure AI use in general.*
- **Adoption gap**: Global North 23%, Global South 13% (10 pp difference) ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/04/04/ai-adoption-and-inequality-565729)) *Corrected November 1, 2026: These numbers are from Microsoft's telemetry for H1 2025 (22.9% and 13.1%), not the IMF paper cited here ([Microsoft, May 7, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Microsoft-AI-Diffusion-Report-2026-Q1.pdf)).*
- **Economic impact**: AI growth in advanced economies projected >2x that of low-income countries ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/04/04/ai-adoption-and-inequality-565729)) *Corrected November 1, 2026: This is a model estimate from a different IMF paper: the growth impact in advanced economies "could be more than double" that in low-income countries ([IMF WP 2025/076, Apr 11, 2025](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/04/11/the-global-impact-of-ai-mind-the-gap-566129)).*
- **Emerging leaders**: India, Brazil, Mexico, South Africa have highest adoption rates despite lower absolute usage ([Modern Diplomacy](https://moderndiplomacy.eu/2025/03/04/why-is-ai-adoption-slower-in-the-global-south-and-how-can-it-leap-forward/))
- **Infrastructure barriers**: 25% of Asia-Pacific offline, women in South Asia 40% less likely to own smartphones ([OECD-Cisco](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/oecd-cisco-research-finds-stark-geographical-and-generational-divides-in-ai-uptake-and-digital-well-being-302632418.html))
- **Speed vs equity**: AI adoption happening in months (not decades), many countries lack infrastructure/skills/governance ([UN](https://www.euronews.com/next/2025/12/02/ai-race-is-faster-than-countries-can-adapt-threatening-greater-global-inequality-un-report)) *Corrected November 1, 2026: This finding was cited through news coverage. The primary source is the [UNDP report, Dec 2025](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-12/undp-rbap-the-next-great-divergence.pdf).*
