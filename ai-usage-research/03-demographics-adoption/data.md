# Demographics & Adoption: Detailed Data

## November 2026 evidence

*Updated November 1, 2026. The November 2026 section is current. Everything after it is the December 2025 compilation, kept as dated history. Superlatives and words like "latest", "leading" or "now" in that older text describe December 2025.*

- Gallup's American Job Quality Study found U.S. college graduates were more than twice as likely as workers without a degree to use AI daily or weekly (40% vs 17%), and 37% of managers vs 25% of individual contributors. ([Gallup, Oct 6, 2026](https://news.gallup.com/poll/714602/benefits-work-unevenly-distributed.aspx)) *Method: Fielded 2026-01-26 to 2026-03-24, 15,482 U.S. employees (sample of 17,874 working adults aged 18-75), margin of error plus or minus 1 point.*
- The St. Louis Fed's Real-Time Population Survey shows the share of U.S. adults aged 18 to 64 using generative AI rose from 45% (August 2024) to 62% (May 2026), and the authors say age, education and sex explain little of why some workers use AI for more tasks than others. ([St. Louis Fed, Sep 1, 2026](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2026/sep/what-work-does-generative-ai-do)) *Method: Real-Time Population Survey, nationally representative, U.S. adults 18-64.*
- Gallup's Q2 2026 workforce survey found 52% of U.S. workers use AI in their role, 30% frequently and 15% daily. ([Gallup, Jul 20, 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/712736/organizational-adoption-jumps-six-points.aspx)) *Method: Gallup Panel, employed U.S. adults, fielded 2026-05-06 to 2026-05-20, n=22,573, margin of error plus or minus 0.9 points.*
- Pew found about half of U.S. adults (49%) said in February 2026 that they use AI chatbots such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot, up from about a third (33%) in summer 2024. ([Pew Research Center, Jun 17, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)) *Method: Pew American Trends Panel, 5,119 U.S. adults, fielded Feb 17-23, 2026. The 2024 question wording differed, so the rise from 33% has a wording break.*
- Pew found 44% of U.S. adults ever use ChatGPT in February 2026, up from 34% in 2025, and 57% of adults under 50 versus 28% of those 50 and older. ([Pew Research Center, Jun 17, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)) *Method: Pew American Trends Panel Wave 187, 5,119 U.S. adults, fielded 2026-02-17 to 2026-02-23, margin of sampling error plus or minus 1.6 points, response rate 87%.*
- Pew found 63% of U.S. adults under 50 use AI chatbots versus about four in ten of those 50 to 64 and fewer of those 65 and older, and a third of adults under 50 use them daily (February 2026). ([Pew Research Center, Jun 17, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/how-opinions-and-use-of-ai-differ-by-age/)) *Method: Pew American Trends Panel Wave 187, 5,119 U.S. adults, fielded 2026-02-17 to 2026-02-23, margin of sampling error plus or minus 1.6 points, response rate 87%.*
- Pew found the gender gap in overall U.S. adult chatbot use has closed: it was 11 percentage points (men more likely) two years earlier; men and women now report 50% and 47% ever use, but men use daily more (27% vs 20%). ([Pew Research Center, Jun 17, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/the-gender-gap-in-ai/)) *Method: Pew American Trends Panel Wave 187, 5,119 U.S. adults, fielded 2026-02-17 to 2026-02-23, margin of sampling error plus or minus 1.6 points, response rate 87%.*
- Pew found 70% of Asian American adults use AI chatbots versus roughly half or fewer of Hispanic, Black and White adults, and roughly half of Asian adults use them daily (February 2026). ([Pew Research Center, Jun 17, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/racial-and-ethnic-differences-in-how-adults-use-and-view-ai/)) *Method: Pew American Trends Panel Wave 187, 5,119 U.S. adults, fielded 2026-02-17 to 2026-02-23, margin of sampling error plus or minus 1.6 points, response rate 87%.*
- The Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026 found 17% of the youngest age group use AI chatbots for news weekly versus 5% of the oldest, and 10% globally, up from 7% a year earlier. ([Reuters Institute, Jun 16, 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/emerging-uses-ai-chatbots-news-and-what-it-means-journalism)) *Method: Online survey across about 48 markets (45 markets for some items), weekly use of AI chatbots for news.*
- OpenAI reported in May 2026 that users with typically feminine names represented a growing share of ChatGPT usage on consumer plans. OpenAI infers this from names; it does not measure gender. ([OpenAI, May 11, 2026](https://openai.com/signals/research/2026q1-update/)) *Method: Vendor-reported.*
- Pew's February 2026 follow-up on the same teen survey found about half of parents say their teen uses chatbots while 64% of teens say they do, so parent reports understate teen use. ([Pew Research Center, Feb 24, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/)) *Method: Same Pew teen survey as the December 2025 report (1,458 teens, fielded 2025-09-25 to 2025-10-09).*
- OECD data for 2025 show more than one third of individuals across the OECD used generative AI tools, with a 53.6 point age gap, about 21 point gaps by education and by income, and a 4.2 point gender gap. ([OECD, Jan 28, 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/announcements/2026/01/ai-use-by-individuals-surges-across-the-oecd-as-adoption-by-firms-continues-to-expand)) *Method: Official statistics, OECD countries where data exist, use in the last three months, 2025 or latest year.*
- A UK preprint using 2023-2024 tracker waves (N=9,172) finds men report substantially higher frequent personal GenAI use than women, which contrasts with Pew's 2026 U.S. finding that the overall gap has closed. ([arXiv, Jan 7, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03880)) *Method: Preprint by the authors, UK Public Attitudes to Data and AI Tracker, two waves 2023-2024, 'frequent personal use', not 'ever use'.*
- Pew's teen survey (fielded 2025-09-25 to 2025-10-09) found 64% of U.S. teens use AI chatbots and about three in ten use them daily; Black teens (35%) and Hispanic teens (33%) use them daily more than White teens (22%). ([Pew Research Center, Dec 9, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/12/09/teens-social-media-and-ai-chatbots-2025/)) *Method: 1,458 U.S. teens 13-17 surveyed online via Ipsos, 2025-09-25 to 2025-10-09, margin of error plus or minus 3.3 points.*
- The OECD's own December 2025 brief on its Cisco poll (about 1,000 respondents per country in 14 countries, early 2025) says generative AI adoption is highest among 18-35 year olds with India, Brazil and South Africa leading and Germany, France and Italy lower; the brief warns the results need caution because of the poll's nature. ([OECD, Dec 4, 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/how-do-people-experience-new-technologies-and-generative-ai_49b8d10e-en/full-report.html)) *Method: OECD WISE Centre poll with Cisco.*
- Stanford's 2026 AI Index public-opinion chapter says that in 2025, 58% of employees globally reported using AI at work semiregularly or regularly, with the share above 80% in India, China, Nigeria, the UAE, Egypt and Saudi Arabia. ([Stanford HAI, undated page, read Nov 1, 2026](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/public-opinion))

## Gender Demographics

### ChatGPT - Dramatic Shift to Gender Parity

**Two Different Methodologies Show Similar Gender Parity:**

**General Mid-2025 Data (from statistical aggregators):**
- Male: 54.66%
- Female: 45.34%
- **Nearly balanced distribution**

**[OpenAI-Harvard Study](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/) (1.5M conversation analysis):**
- Female: 52.4% (slight majority)
- Male: 47.6%

*Note: Both statistics are accurate but from different sources and methodologies. Both demonstrate achievement of gender parity, a dramatic shift from January 2024.*

**Historical Context:**
- January 2024: Male-dominated (only 37% had typically feminine names)
- **Interpretation**: Dramatic 12-month shift toward gender parity

**Significance:**
- Faster gender parity than previous tech waves (social media, smartphones)
- Suggests broad accessibility and appeal
- Early adopter bias has dissipated

### Gemini
- Higher male usage (specific percentage not provided in sources)
- Less gender parity than ChatGPT

### Teen AI Users (All Platforms, Pew Research)
- **Gender parity**: Boys and girls equally likely to use AI chatbots
- No significant gender gap among 13-17 year olds

**Interpretation:**
- Younger cohorts show no gender divide
- Gender gaps appear to be age-dependent, not platform-dependent

- Less engaged with AI
- More uncertain about benefits/risks
- Lower adoption rates

**Significance:**
- Starkest divide is age, not gender or education
- Suggests generational shift in technology comfort

## Geographic & Global Patterns

### Income-Based Adoption Paradox

**[OpenAI Study](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/) Finding (by May 2025):**
- Growth rate in lowest income countries: **4x that of highest income countries**
- Suggests rapid adoption in developing economies

**Emerging Economy Leadership:**
- India, Brazil, Mexico, South Africa: **Highest adoption rates globally**
- Highest trust levels in AI
- Most active engagement
- **Departure from historical technology adoption patterns**

**Traditional Pattern**: Wealthy countries adopt first, poor countries lag
**AI Pattern**: Emerging economies adopting fastest

**Possible Explanations:**
1. Leapfrogging effect (skipping intermediate tech)
2. Lack of legacy infrastructure to replace
3. Greater perceived utility in under-resourced contexts
4. Mobile-first populations
5. Educational gaps AI helps bridge

### Regional Growth - Gemini Specific

**Global South Leaders:**
- India and Brazil lead Gemini user growth
- 22% of new account activations (2025)
- Africa: 180% year-over-year growth

**Interpretation:**
- Multiple platforms seeing emerging market strength
- Not just OpenAI phenomenon
- Sustainable trend across frontier labs

### Geographic Inequality Despite Growth

**[Anthropic AI Usage Index](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report) (AUI):**
- Singapore: 4.6x expected usage
- Canada: 2.9x expected usage
- India: 0.27x expected usage
- Nigeria: 0.20x expected usage

**Interpretation:**
- **Singapore uses AI 23x more than Nigeria** (4.6 / 0.20)
- High growth in poor countries doesn't mean high absolute usage
- Gap between rich and poor countries remains severe

### The AI Divide ([IMF Research](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/04/04/ai-adoption-and-inequality-565729))

**Adoption Rates:**
- Global North: 23%
- Global South: 13%
- **10 percentage point gap**

**Economic Impact Projections:**
- AI growth impact in advanced economies: **>2x that of low-income countries**
- Warning: Could reverse decades of narrowing development inequalities

### Infrastructure & Access Barriers

**Asia-Pacific:**
- 25% of population remains offline
- No internet access = no AI access

**Gender-Geography Intersection:**
- Women in South Asia: Up to 40% less likely to own smartphone than men
- Compounds access barriers

**UN Development Programme Warning:**
- AI adoption happening in months (not decades like previous tech)
- Many countries lack infrastructure, skills, governance systems
- Risk of widening global inequality despite rapid emerging market growth

## Adoption Curves & Diffusion

### Speed of Adoption

**ChatGPT:**
- Fastest-growing consumer app in history
- 100M users in 2 months (launch to milestone)
- Now 700-800M WAU (sustained explosive growth)

**Gemini:**
- 7M → 82M MAU in ~18 months
- Rapid growth from integrated Google base

**Interpretation:**
- AI adoption faster than smartphone, social media, internet
- Network effects + utility driving acceleration
- Different from typical S-curve adoption

### Diffusion Patterns

**Traditional (e.g., Internet):**
1. Wealthy countries first
2. Urban before rural
3. Young before old
4. Male before female

**AI Diffusion (Observed):**
1. ✅ Young before old (confirmed)
2. ❌ Male before female (NO - gender parity achieved quickly)
3. ❌ Wealthy countries first (PARTIALLY - emerging economies growing fastest)
4. ❓ Urban before rural (data unavailable)

**Unique Characteristic:**
- Faster gender parity than any previous major technology
- Simultaneous global adoption (not sequential by country income)

## Data Gaps & Research Needs

### Missing Demographics
1. **Detailed age brackets**: Granular breakdowns only for ChatGPT
2. **Race/ethnicity (adults)**: Teen data available, adult data scarce
3. **Education level**: College vs non-college breakdown
4. **Income**: Individual user income data unavailable
5. **Urban/rural**: No platform reporting this split
6. **Occupation**: Beyond broad industry categories

### Missing Cross-Platform Data
1. **Multi-homing**: What % use multiple platforms?
2. **Primary platform**: Which is "main" vs supplementary?
3. **Platform switching**: Migration patterns between platforms
4. **Demographic sorting**: Do different demographics cluster on different platforms?

### Geographic Gaps
1. **Country-specific breakdowns**: Limited data beyond major markets
2. **Regional patterns**: Within-country variation unexplored
3. **Global South detail**: Aggregated data, need country-level granularity

### Longitudinal Gaps
1. **Cohort analysis**: Are early adopters still active?
2. **Churn rates**: Who stops using AI tools?
3. **Adoption maturity**: What happens after initial trial?

## Summary: Demographic Revolution

### What's Different About AI Adoption

1. **Gender Parity Speed**: Achieved in ~12 months (vs years for smartphones)
2. **Emerging Market Leadership**: Growth rates 4x higher in poor countries
3. **Youth Dominance**: But not exclusively young (32% are 35-54)
4. **Racial Pattern Flip**: U.S. minority teens adopting faster than white teens
5. **Cross-Industry**: 72% of companies across all sectors

### What's Similar to Other Tech

1. **Age Divide**: Still youth-skewed, older adults lagging
2. **Absolute Inequality**: Rich countries/areas still use more in absolute terms
3. **STEM Overrepresentation**: Technical fields adopting first
4. **Urban Likely Ahead**: Though data unavailable, pattern probably holds

### The Paradox

- **Growth**: Fastest in poor countries
- **Usage**: Highest in rich countries
- **Gap**: Potentially widening despite rapid emerging market adoption

**Critical Implication**: Speed doesn't equal equity. Fast growth in under-resourced areas may still leave them behind in absolute terms.
